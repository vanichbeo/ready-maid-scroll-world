Find a Baby or Child Care Helper for Your Family

Ready Maid Agency is operated by Agensi Pekerjaan Expert Sdn. Bhd. · Licence C · JTKSM 432

Ready Maid helps Malaysian families review and interview domestic-helper candidates for newborn care, baby care, toddler care and support for older children. A suitable child-care helper should be selected according to actual experience, safety awareness, patience, hygiene, communication and ability to follow the parents’ routine.

What Is a Baby and Child Care Helper?

A baby and child care helper is a domestic helper whose agreed responsibilities include supporting the daily care of a baby or child. Duties may include milk preparation, bottle cleaning, diaper changing, bathing, baby-food preparation, feeding, sleep routines, safe play, child-related laundry, school preparation and parent updates.

A domestic helper should not automatically be presented as a trained nanny, nurse or early-childhood educator unless her qualifications and experience are properly verified.

Types of Child-Care Roles

Newborn care

Frequent feeding, bottle preparation, diapers, hygiene, sleep monitoring, night assistance and careful reporting.

Infant care

Feeding, baby food, bathing, diapers, naps, safe play, mobility supervision and cleaning feeding equipment.

Toddler care

Meals, bathing, toileting, safe play, outdoor supervision, nap routine, toy cleaning and emotional support.

School-age child support

Morning preparation, meals, school bags, transport support, after-school routine and homework supervision according to parental instructions.

What Makes a Good Baby-Care Candidate?

  • Good hygiene and safe handling
  • Patience and calmness
  • Age-specific experience
  • Ability to follow instructions
  • Willingness to report mistakes
  • Attention to detail
  • Emotional stability
  • Clear communication

Questions to Verify Newborn Experience

  1. How old was the youngest baby you cared for?
  2. Were you the main caregiver?
  3. Did you prepare formula or expressed milk?
  4. How did you clean and sterilise bottles?
  5. Did you change diapers and bathe the baby?
  6. Did you assist at night?
  7. How did you settle the baby to sleep?
  8. What did you do when the baby cried continuously?
  9. How did you report feeding and diaper changes?
  10. What would you do after a fall, fever or injury?

Safety, Feeding and Hygiene

Baby safety is more important than speed. Employers should demonstrate safe holding, feeding position, bottle hygiene, bathing, sleep instructions, chemical storage, small-object hazards and emergency reporting.

The helper should not independently alter milk type, formula ratio, feeding quantity, feeding schedule, medicine or supplements. She should not introduce new food without parental approval.

Parents should demonstrate handwashing, bottle cleaning, surface cleaning, diaper disposal, baby-clothing separation, feeding-equipment cleaning and safe food storage.

Night Duties and Rest

Night care must be discussed before confirmation. Clarify whether the helper sleeps near the baby, how often night assistance is expected, who handles feeding and how daytime rest will be adjusted. Repeated night work without recovery can increase safety risks.

Play, Discipline and Parent Communication

A helper may support reading, songs, safe floor play, drawing, puzzles, supervised outdoor activities and simple language practice. Parents should set rules for screens, outdoor areas, photos, snacks and discipline.

Physical punishment, threats, humiliation, locking a child alone, withholding food and frightening the child are unacceptable. The helper should follow the parents’ approved approach.

Daily updates may include feeding, diapers, sleep, meals, mood, activities, medicine given according to instructions, accidents and unusual symptoms.

What Must Be Reported Immediately?

  • Falls, choking or breathing difficulty
  • High fever, repeated vomiting or serious diarrhoea
  • Unusual sleepiness, seizure or allergic reaction
  • Injury, poisoning concern or missing medicine
  • Any situation the helper cannot understand or manage safely

Nationality and Experience

A Filipino candidate may be considered when English communication is important. An Indonesian candidate may be considered when Bahasa Melayu or Bahasa Indonesia communication is more suitable. Nationality alone does not determine safety, patience, honesty or baby-care skill.

First-time candidates may be motivated and flexible but need more training. Experienced candidates may be more confident, but their experience must still match the child’s age and the family’s routine.

Workload and Two-Helper Households

When the baby requires attention, non-essential household tasks may need to wait. A realistic priority order is baby safety, feeding and hygiene, essential meals, essential cleaning and then additional duties.

Families with twins, several children, intensive elderly care, significant night duties or a large household may need one primary baby-care helper and one primary household-support helper.

How Ready Maid Reviews Child-Care Candidates

  • Age of children previously cared for
  • Length of experience and whether she was the main caregiver
  • Feeding, bottle hygiene, diapers and bathing
  • Sleep routine and night duties
  • Child-related cooking
  • Communication and safety awareness
  • Interview consistency, attitude and family expectations

Information Families Should Share

  • Location and household members
  • Child’s age and number of children
  • Main care duties and night-care expectations
  • Whether parents or grandparents are present
  • Cooking, housework, pets and language needs
  • Desired starting date

First-Week Training

Day 1 should focus on rest, family introduction, home orientation, sleeping area and essential safety rules. Days 2–3 should demonstrate feeding, bottle cleaning, diapers, bathing, sleep routine, laundry and reporting. Days 4–7 should involve supervised practice and correction.
