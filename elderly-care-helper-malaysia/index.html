Ready Maid Commercial Service Guide

Find an Elderly-Care Helper for Your Family

Ready Maid Agency is operated by Agensi Pekerjaan Expert Sdn. Bhd. · Licence C · JTKSM 432

Ready Maid helps Malaysian families review and interview domestic-helper candidates for elderly companionship, mobility assistance, personal care, meal preparation and household support. The right candidate should be selected according to the elderly person’s condition, duties required, relevant experience, patience, physical ability, communication and willingness to follow the family’s instructions.

Types of Elderly-Care Needs

Independent elderly person

Companionship, cooking, light cleaning, grocery support, appointment accompaniment, medication reminders and general observation.

Light assistance

Walking supervision, bathroom support, meal preparation, dressing assistance, reminders and support on stairs.

Limited mobility

Wheelchair support, transfers, bathing, toileting, diaper changing, repositioning, meal support and night monitoring.

Bedridden care

Personal hygiene, diaper changing, repositioning according to instructions, feeding assistance, bedside support and reporting changes.

Memory or behavioural concerns

Patience, calm communication, close supervision, consistent routines, safety instructions and immediate reporting.

Domestic Helper or Nurse?

A domestic helper may assist with daily living and household routines but should not independently diagnose conditions, change medicine, alter dosage, perform unverified medical procedures or replace a qualified nurse, therapist or doctor.

What Makes a Good Elderly-Care Candidate?

  • Patience and respect
  • Calm communication
  • Relevant care experience
  • Physical ability for the agreed duties
  • Good hygiene and safety awareness
  • Comfort with personal-care duties
  • Reliability and honest reporting
  • Emotional stability

Questions to Verify Experience

  1. How old was the elderly person?
  2. Could the person walk independently?
  3. Did the person use a walking frame or wheelchair?
  4. Was the person bedridden?
  5. Did you assist with bathing, toileting or diapers?
  6. Did you help with bed-to-chair transfers?
  7. Did you prepare special meals?
  8. Did you assist at night?
  9. Did you remind the person about medication?
  10. What difficult situation did you handle?
  11. Were you the main caregiver?

Communication and Companionship

Consider the elderly person’s language, hearing, anxiety and communication style. An Indonesian candidate may be useful for Malay-speaking elderly family members, while a Filipino candidate may suit English-speaking households. The final decision should still be based on individual experience and personality.

Companionship may include conversation, short walks, simple activities, drinks, meals and reporting changes. The helper should be respectful and should not speak to the elderly person like a child.

Mobility, Transfers and Personal Care

The family should demonstrate safe walking support, wheelchair brakes, footrests, ramps, bathroom access and approved transfer methods. Heavy transfers may require two people, equipment or professional guidance.

Bathing and toileting should protect privacy and dignity. Personal-care duties must be disclosed before candidate confirmation.

Meals, Feeding and Medication Reminders

Families should provide written instructions for soft food, low-salt or low-sugar meals, food texture, hydration and dietary restrictions. A helper should not independently change a medically advised diet.

Medication reminders should follow a family-prepared system. The helper must not change medicine, increase or reduce dosage, give unapproved remedies or stop medicine.

Night Care and Reporting

Clarify whether the elderly person wakes frequently, needs toileting help, uses diapers, may wander or requires the helper to sleep nearby. Repeated night work requires reasonable daytime recovery.

Daily updates may cover meals, fluids, toileting, sleep, mobility, mood, medication reminders, pain, falls, skin changes and unusual behaviour.

Immediate Reporting

  • Falls, breathing difficulty or chest pain
  • Loss of consciousness, sudden weakness or seizure
  • Serious bleeding, choking or suspected stroke symptoms
  • High fever, repeated vomiting or refusal of food and water
  • Severe confusion, major behaviour change or missing medication
  • Any situation the helper cannot manage safely

Workload and Two-Helper Support

Intensive elderly care may not be compatible with full heavy housework. Families may need additional family involvement, part-time cleaning, professional support or a second helper.

A clear two-helper structure may assign one helper primary elderly-care responsibility and another primary cooking and household responsibility.

How Ready Maid Reviews Elderly-Care Candidates

  • The elderly person’s previous age and condition
  • Mobility, wheelchair and bedridden-care experience
  • Bathing, toileting and diaper changing
  • Feeding, cooking and medication reminders
  • Night duties, communication and physical ability
  • Patience, interview consistency and family expectations

Information Families Should Share

  • Location and household members
  • Elderly person’s age and mobility
  • Wheelchair, bedridden or diaper status
  • Bathing, feeding and night-care requirements
  • Language, house type and other household duties
  • Desired starting date

First-Week Training

Day 1 should cover rest, family introduction, elderly routine, sleeping area, bathroom arrangement, safety rules and emergency contacts. Days 2–3 should demonstrate mobility support, wheelchair use, bathing, toileting, meals, medication reminders and reporting. Days 4–7 should involve supervised practice.
