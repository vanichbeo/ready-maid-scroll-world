Direct answer

Choose an elderly-care helper according to the elderly person’s actual condition and the candidate’s relevant experience, patience, communication, physical ability, safety awareness and willingness to follow family instructions. Clarify mobility, bathing, toileting, personal care, night assistance and medication-reminder duties before the interview so the candidate can confirm whether she is comfortable and physically suited to the role.

Start with the elderly person’s actual condition

Clarify age, mobility, walking aid or wheelchair use, transfers, bathing, toileting, diapers, medication reminders, memory loss, night assistance, special meals, language and other household duties.

What experience should families look for?

Ask about similar care responsibilities, whether the candidate was the main caregiver, actual physical or personal-care duties, length and recency of experience, and whether the experience matches the biodata.

Eight most important selection factors

  • Patience and emotional stability
  • Communication ability
  • Physical ability
  • Comfort with personal care
  • Safety awareness
  • Ability to follow routines
  • Respect and dignity
  • Household compatibility

Important interview questions

  • Tell us about the elderly person you cared for.
  • Could the person walk independently?
  • Did you assist with bathing, toileting or diapers?
  • Did you help with transfers or wheelchair use?
  • What would you do if the person fell, refused food or became angry?
  • Are you comfortable with personal care and agreed night duties?

Indonesian or Filipino helper for elderly care?

Neither nationality is automatically better. Consider communication, actual elderly-care experience, patience, physical capability, personal-care comfort, safety and family compatibility.

Medication responsibilities

A helper may remind, bring pre-prepared medication and report refusal, but should not change dosage, substitute medicine or make medical decisions.

Night-care expectations

Clarify waking frequency, toileting, wandering, repositioning, sleeping arrangement, family support and daytime rest.

Preparing the home

Prepare clear walkways, non-slip surfaces, stable chairs, accessible walking aids, night lighting, emergency contacts, written routines and safe medication storage.

Warning signs

  • Cannot explain claimed care experience
  • Uncomfortable with personal care
  • Underestimates physical duties
  • Shows impatience
  • Says she would hide a fall or mistake
  • Makes independent medical claims
  • Gives conflicting employment details

Frequently asked questions

Is a domestic helper suitable for elderly care?

Yes, when her experience and abilities match the elderly person’s needs. Complex medical or nursing requirements may require qualified professional support.

Can a helper lift an elderly person?

It depends on size, mobility, equipment, physical ability and safe technique.

Can the helper manage medication?

She may assist with reminders or clear family instructions, but should not make medical decisions.

Is a domestic helper a replacement for a nurse?

No. A domestic helper may support daily living, companionship and household routines, but complex nursing, rehabilitation or medical procedures may require qualified professional care.
