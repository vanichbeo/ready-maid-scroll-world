Direct answer

The domestic-helper application process normally begins with checking employer eligibility and household needs, followed by candidate selection and interview, document preparation, submission to relevant authorities, immigration and source-country processing, travel arrangements, arrival clearance and onboarding. Exact requirements and timing vary by nationality, application route and current government procedures, so employers should rely on the latest written checklist and quotation.

Overview of the journey

  • Confirm family requirements
  • Check employer eligibility
  • Review suitable candidates
  • Interview and select a candidate
  • Explain fees, terms and responsibilities
  • Prepare employer and candidate documents
  • Submit applications
  • Complete source-country, embassy and immigration requirements
  • Arrange travel after approval
  • Complete arrival and entry procedures
  • Begin household orientation
  • Complete post-arrival formalities

Stage 1 — Understand the family’s requirements

Gather location, household members, children’s ages, baby or elderly-care needs, main duties, cooking, house type, preferred nationality, language and desired starting period.

Stage 2 — Check employer eligibility

Review nationality or residency status, age, household income, family circumstances, number of helpers, intended nationality and other immigration or government conditions.

Stage 3 — Review suitable candidates

Consider employment history, household size, baby or elderly-care experience, cooking, language, personality, willingness, salary expectation, availability and document consistency.

Stage 4 — Interview the candidate

Discuss previous employers, daily routine, care experience, cooking, communication, difficult situations, unwilling duties, household members, accommodation, rest and night duties.

Stage 5 — Confirm candidate and terms

Clarify identity, nationality, duties, household, salary, rest, employment period, agency fees, payment stages, cancellation and replacement terms, documents and government-dependent conditions.

Stage 6 — Prepare employer documents

Typical documents may include identity documents, proof of income, marriage certificate, children’s birth certificates, medical information, address evidence, forms and employment contract.

Stage 7 — Prepare candidate documents

These may include passport, civil-status documents, employment records, medical results, training records, source-country approvals, contract, visa and insurance.

Stage 8 — Submit to relevant authorities

The process may involve JTKSM, Malaysian Immigration, source-country authorities, embassies, medical providers and insurance providers.

Stage 9 — Immigration and entry approval

This may include application review, supporting-document verification, visa processing, entry arrangements, bonds, fees and insurance.

Stage 10 — Source-country and embassy requirements

These may involve contract verification, medical examination, training, exit clearance, insurance, orientation and registration.

Stage 11 — Medical and insurance requirements

Medical or insurance requirements can affect whether the process continues.

Stage 12 — Travel and arrival

Verify name, passport validity, visa, flight, arrival airport, receiving contact and required documents.

Stage 13 — Post-arrival formalities

These may include medical examination, permit endorsement, insurance confirmation, reporting and record keeping.

Stage 14 — Household orientation

Explain family members, daily schedule, care routines, cooking, cleaning priorities, safety rules, emergency contacts, food, rest and communication.

Stage 15 — Adjustment period

Demonstrate, observe, correct early, keep duties realistic and contact the agency when needed.

What can delay the application?

Incomplete documents, inconsistent names, expired passports, poor scans, missing signatures, candidate withdrawal, medical issues, embassy delays, immigration queries, system interruptions, public holidays and policy changes.

Can an agency guarantee approval or arrival date?

No. Approval and timing depend on authorities and third parties.

Payment safety checklist

Confirm legal company, licence, candidate identity, services, included/excluded costs, payment stages, cancellation terms, replacement terms and receipt arrangements.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to choose a candidate first?

In most placement cases, yes, before candidate-specific processing proceeds.

Can I apply without an agency?

Some routes may permit direct applications, while others require licensed-agency involvement. Verify current rules.

How long does the process take?

There is no guaranteed universal period.

What happens if the candidate withdraws?

Reselection, transfer of eligible documents, additional costs or cancellation may apply.
