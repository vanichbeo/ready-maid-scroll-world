Your First 24 Hours with a New Domestic Helper

The first 24 hours are critical for setting expectations and building rapport with your new domestic helper. Focus on a structured orientation, clear communication of household rules and routines, and creating a welcoming environment. This initial period establishes the foundation for a smooth, long-term working relationship and helps your helper settle in confidently.

Need help with onboarding?

Why the First Day Sets the Tone

The initial 24 hours create the blueprint for your ongoing relationship. A positive, clear start reduces anxiety for both you and your helper, minimizes early misunderstandings, and establishes mutual respect. In the Malaysian context, where domestic helpers often live with their employers, this first impression is particularly significant for household harmony.

Preparing Your Home Before Arrival

Before your helper arrives, ensure her personal space is clean and ready. Stock basic necessities like towels, soap, and bedding. Prepare a clear list of key household contacts, Wi-Fi password, and a simple house map. If possible, have a family photo or small welcome item in her room. These gestures communicate care and reduce first-day disorientation.

A Structured Orientation Schedule

Use a simple schedule: Morning - Formal welcome and introductions to all family members and key rooms (kitchen, laundry, her bedroom). Late Morning - Guided tour focusing on her primary duties area, demonstrating key appliances (washing machine, stove, rice cooker). Afternoon - Sit down together to review the written duty roster, discuss daily routines (meal times, childcare schedules, cleaning priorities), and answer questions. Evening - A brief check-in and an early night to allow for adjustment. Keep explanations simple and visual.

Essential Tools for Clear Communication

Use visual aids: label key items in the kitchen and laundry with both English and Bahasa terms if helpful. Create a simple chart or notebook for daily tasks and important notes. Agree on a routine for daily 5-minute check-ins to address questions. Ensure she has your phone number for emergencies. These tools prevent frustration and empower your helper to work independently sooner.

Building Rapport and Managing Expectations

Be patient and kind during the orientation. Share a little about your family’s daily life to help her feel part of the household. Clearly state household rules regarding guests, phone use, and time off, framing them as standard practice. A positive, respectful tone builds trust, while clear boundaries prevent future issues. Remember, her comfort and confidence directly impact the quality of her work.
