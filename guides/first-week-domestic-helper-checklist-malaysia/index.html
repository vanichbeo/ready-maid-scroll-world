First-Week Checklist for a New Domestic Helper in Malaysia

Use the first week to teach priorities, routines and communication gradually. Start with the household orientation and the most important care or daily duties, demonstrate how your family wants key tasks done, check understanding, and review what needs adjusting before adding more work.

Before the helper arrives

  • Write down the three most important responsibilities.
  • Decide who gives day-to-day instructions so directions are consistent.
  • Prepare sleeping space and personal storage.
  • Prepare emergency contacts and important household numbers.
  • Write down any child, elderly-person or pet routines that must not be forgotten.
  • Choose a simple way for the helper to ask questions and give updates.

Day 1: orient, do not overload

  • Give a calm tour of the home and storage areas.
  • Introduce family members and explain who needs priority attention.
  • Explain meal times, wake-up times and the normal family rhythm.
  • Show the safest way to use important appliances and cleaning products.
  • Give only the most important tasks for the first day.

Days 2–3: demonstrate and check understanding

Show the most important recurring tasks instead of relying only on verbal instructions. Explain the expected result, demonstrate the steps, then ask the helper to explain the routine back in her own words. A simple question such as “What will you do first tomorrow morning?” is more useful than asking only “Do you understand?”

Days 4–5: increase responsibility gradually

Once the basic routine is understood, allow more independent execution while still checking the highest-priority areas. Avoid adding many optional jobs before the essential childcare, elderly support, cooking or household duties are stable.

Days 6–7: review and simplify

  • What is clear now?
  • Which tasks are still confusing?
  • Which part of the routine takes too long?
  • Are priorities realistic for the household?
  • Which instructions should be written down?
  • What should change for week two?

Use a two-minute daily check-in

Ask three short questions: “What was difficult today?”, “Is anything unclear for tomorrow?”, and “Is there anything important I should know?” This gives both employer and helper a simple way to surface small problems before they become repeated habits.

Common first-week mistakes

  • Giving too many instructions at once.
  • Different family members giving conflicting instructions.
  • Assuming silence means understanding.
  • Expecting full speed before the helper knows the home.
  • Correcting every small detail while ignoring task priority.
  • Adding childcare, cooking and deep cleaning without deciding what comes first.

First-week checklist

  • Household tour completed
  • Family priorities explained
  • Emergency contacts shared
  • Daily routine demonstrated
  • Key tasks written down
  • Understanding checked
  • Questions encouraged
  • Daily review completed
  • Week-two adjustments agreed

When to ask for support

If the difficulty appears to be more than normal unfamiliarity with the home—for example a possible mismatch in experience, communication or job expectations—contact Ready Maid so the situation can be reviewed early.
