Domestic Helper Interview Questions

A good domestic helper interview should assess the candidate’s actual experience, communication, attitude, expectations and suitability for your household. Direct answer

A good domestic helper interview should assess the candidate’s actual experience, communication, attitude, expectations and suitability for your household. Ask practical questions about previous duties, baby or elderly care, cooking, difficult situations and daily routines. Avoid choosing based only on confidence, nationality or years of experience.

Prepare before the interview

Agree on who lives in the household, the helper’s main duties, whether baby, child or elderly care is required, and the expected starting period. Also clarify house size, cooking, language, pets, sleeping arrangement, rest day, physical duties and special routines.

20 important interview questions

  • Please introduce yourself and tell us about your family.
  • Tell us about your previous employer.
  • What were your main daily duties?
  • Why did you leave your previous employer?
  • What did you enjoy most in your previous job?
  • What was the most difficult part of your previous job?
  • What would you do if you did not understand an instruction?
  • How do you organise your daily work?
  • What dishes can you cook?
  • How would you learn a new recipe?
  • Have you cared for a baby before?
  • What would you do if a baby would not stop crying?
  • What would you do if a child refused to eat?
  • How would you keep a baby or child safe?
  • Have you cared for an elderly person before?
  • What would you do if an elderly person refused your help?
  • Are you comfortable assisting someone with walking, bathing or toileting?
  • What type of employer and household do you prefer?
  • Are there duties you are not willing or able to perform?
  • What are your expectations from your employer?

Follow-up questions that reveal more

  • Can you give me an example?
  • What happened next?
  • How often did you perform that duty?
  • Who taught you?
  • What would you do differently now?
  • Was anyone else helping you?
  • How old was the child?
  • How mobile was the elderly person?

What employers should observe

Consistency

Compare answers with biodata, employment dates, household details, duties and reasons for leaving.

Communication

Observe whether the candidate understands, asks for clarification and explains experience clearly.

Attitude

Look for respectful communication, willingness to learn, realistic expectations and honesty about limitations.

Emotional response

Observe whether the candidate becomes defensive, blames everyone else, explains calmly and accepts responsibility where appropriate.

Questions employers should avoid

Do not pressure the candidate to promise perfection, hide difficult duties, ask frightening questions, make nationality assumptions or promise conditions the family cannot provide.

Common interview mistakes

  • Choosing the most confident speaker
  • Asking only yes-or-no questions
  • Explaining too little about the household
  • Ignoring mismatched expectations
  • Rushing because another employer may select the candidate
  • Expecting perfect answers

Suggested 15-minute interview structure

  • Minutes 1–3: introduction and background
  • Minutes 4–7: experience and daily routine
  • Minutes 8–11: practical scenarios
  • Minutes 12–14: expectations
  • Minute 15: candidate questions

Conclusion

A useful interview should establish whether the candidate has performed similar duties, understands the household’s expectations, and can communicate and work with the family realistically.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a domestic-helper interview take?

A focused interview may take about 15 to 30 minutes, with enough time for experience, practical scenarios, expectations and the candidate’s own questions.

Should I ask only yes-or-no questions?

No. Use open questions and follow-ups such as ‘Can you give an example?’ so the candidate explains what she actually did.

What should I compare with the biodata?

Compare employment dates, household members, children’s ages, elderly conditions, duties, countries worked and reasons for leaving.

Can one interview guarantee a good match?

No. The interview improves understanding, but employers should also review documented experience, expectations, references where available and the full job scope.
