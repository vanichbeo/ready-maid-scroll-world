Baby Care Tasks Your Helper Should Know

You should write down clear, simple instructions for all daily baby care tasks to ensure consistency and safety. Focus on core routines like feeding, sleeping, bathing, and playtime. Include specific details about your baby's preferences, any health notes, and strict safety rules. Providing a written list helps your helper feel confident and ensures your baby's care is always on track.

Why Writing Tasks Down is Essential

Writing down baby care tasks is the foundation for a successful relationship with your helper. It removes guesswork, ensures important routines are followed exactly, and gives your helper the confidence to care for your child independently. For a new domestic helper, especially one new to Malaysia, a clear list is a critical tool for handover and daily success.

Core Daily Routines to Document

Detail your baby's specific schedule and preferences for: <br>• <strong>Feeding:</strong> Times, quantities, type of milk (formula/expressed milk), bottle preparation steps, and solid food introduction rules. <br>• <strong>Sleeping:</strong> Nap times, bedtime routine, sleep environment preferences (white noise, swaddle), and safe sleep guidelines. <br>• <strong>Bathing & Hygiene:</strong> Bath time procedure, water temperature, skincare routine, diaper changing steps, and laundry instructions for baby clothes. <br>• <strong>Play & Development:</strong> Age-appropriate activities, favourite toys, screen time rules, and outdoor time permissions.

Safety Rules & Emergency Procedures

Create a separate, highly visible list for critical safety information. Include: safe sleeping positions (always on back), never leaving baby unattended on high surfaces, kitchen and hot water safety, approved holding techniques, and a clear list of choking hazards. Provide emergency contact numbers for parents, the paediatrician, and the nearest clinic. Explain what to do in case of fever, vomiting, or minor injuries.

Health & Allergy Notes

If your baby has any allergies, medical conditions, or is on medication, write these down clearly. Include instructions for administering any prescribed medicine, signs of an allergic reaction to watch for, and what to avoid. Even if there are no known allergies, a general note like 'Watch for rash after new foods' can be helpful.

Handover Tips for a Smooth Start

During the first week, go through the written list with your helper each morning. Encourage them to ask questions. You can use the list as a guide while you demonstrate tasks. Keep the document in a common area like on the refrigerator. Update it as you notice your baby's routine changing. This proactive approach builds trust and competence from day one.
