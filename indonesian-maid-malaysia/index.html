Is an Indonesian Helper Suitable for Your Family?

An Indonesian helper may be worth considering when your household values:

  • Communication in Bahasa Indonesia or Bahasa Melayu
  • Familiarity with regional food and ingredients
  • Baby or child-care experience
  • Elderly-care experience
  • Cooking and general household support
  • A candidate willing to adapt to Malaysian family routines

Not every Indonesian candidate has the same experience or personality. Two candidates from the same country may differ significantly in previous duties, communication, cooking ability, care experience, working habits, expectations, emotional maturity and adaptability.

Types of Indonesian Helpers

First-time overseas candidates

These candidates may have local household experience, personal child-care experience, elderly-family care experience, cooking and cleaning skills, but limited overseas employment exposure. They may require more guidance after arrival while still being suitable when they understand the role and are willing to learn.

Experienced overseas helpers

Some candidates have worked in Malaysia, Singapore, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Middle Eastern countries or other overseas locations. Overseas experience may be useful, but employers should verify the actual duties performed rather than relying only on the number of years worked.

Transfer candidates

A transfer candidate may already be in Malaysia and seeking a suitable new employer, subject to applicable procedures and approvals. Families should confirm the reason for transfer, documentation status, interview availability, expected start date and any additional requirements.

Possible Duties

  • General housework, cleaning, laundry and ironing
  • Cooking and grocery support
  • Baby and child care
  • Elderly companionship and personal-care assistance
  • Pet care and car washing where disclosed and agreed
  • Other lawful household duties stated before confirmation

Baby and Child Care

An Indonesian candidate may be suitable for baby or child care when she has relevant age-specific experience and demonstrates safe handling, hygiene awareness, patience, feeding and diaper-changing experience, ability to follow the parents’ routine and willingness to learn.

Employers should ask how old the child was, whether the candidate was the main caregiver, what she did each day and whether she assisted at night. The phrase “child-care experience” is too general without these details.

Elderly Care

An Indonesian candidate may be particularly useful when an elderly family member communicates more comfortably in Bahasa Melayu. Language alone is not enough. Families should also assess mobility support, bathing or toileting assistance, diaper changing, wheelchair experience, physical ability, night-care expectations and safety awareness.

Cooking and Household Support

Some Indonesian candidates may be familiar with ingredients and cooking styles similar to Malaysian food. Cooking ability still varies. Ask candidates to name actual dishes, explain what they cooked independently and confirm whether they can follow recipes and learn Chinese, Indian, Western or less-spicy family meals.

Communication

Many Indonesian candidates communicate in Bahasa Indonesia, which may be understood by Malaysian families using Bahasa Melayu. During the interview, assess whether the candidate can understand instructions, explain previous work, ask questions, report problems and learn household terms.

Indonesian or Filipino Helper?

Neither nationality is automatically better. An Indonesian candidate may suit a family that values Bahasa Melayu communication, regional cooking familiarity or candidate-specific elderly-care experience. A Filipino candidate may suit a family that values stronger English communication or candidate-specific child-care and overseas experience. The final decision should be based on the individual candidate.

How Ready Maid Reviews Candidates

  • Employment history and household size
  • Previous duties
  • Baby, child or elderly-care experience
  • Cooking and language
  • Interview consistency
  • Candidate expectations
  • Employer requirements and desired start date
  • Overall family compatibility

Information Families Should Share

  1. Employer’s location
  2. Household members
  3. Main helper duties
  4. Desired starting date

Additional useful information includes children’s ages, the elderly person’s mobility, house type, cooking expectations, preferred language, pets, night duties and any existing helper arrangement.

Candidate Interview and Verification

Families should normally interview the candidate before confirmation. Discuss previous employers, household members, daily routine, care experience, cooking, communication, difficult situations, reason for leaving, duties she is not comfortable performing, salary, rest expectations and her questions about the family.

Before confirming, verify the candidate’s name, age, passport details where available, employment dates, countries worked, household details, main duties, reason for leaving, salary expectations and availability.

First-Time or Experienced Candidate?

A first-time candidate may be open to learning and may have fewer fixed habits, but usually needs more training and emotional support. An experienced candidate may understand overseas household routines and require less basic training, but her previous experience may not match the new household and her working preferences may be more established.
